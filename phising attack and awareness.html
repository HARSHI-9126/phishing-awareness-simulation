What is Phishing?

A social engineering attack that tricks users into revealing sensitive information

Understanding the Threat

Phishing is a cyberattack method where attackers impersonate legitimate organizations through email, text, or phone calls to deceive users into:

  • Clicking malicious links
  • Downloading malware-infected attachments
  • Entering passwords or financial information
  • Transferring money to fraudulent accounts
  • Providing personal or corporate data

Why it works: Phishing exploits human psychology and trust rather than technical vulnerabilities, making it difficult to defend against with technology alone.

⚠️ Red Flags: Urgent tone, misspelled domain, generic greeting

Red Flags to Recognize

Learn the warning signs that indicate a phishing attempt

⚠️

Urgent Language

"Verify immediately," "Account will be closed," "Confirm within 24 hours" — pressure tactics to bypass your critical thinking

🔗

Suspicious Links

Hover over links to see the real URL. Mismatched text and destination URLs are a major red flag. Watch for slight domain variations.

📧

Unfamiliar Sender

Check the sender's email address carefully. Legitimate companies won't ask sensitive info via email. Domain spoofing is common.

💰

Financial Requests

Requests for payment, bank details, or credit card information. Banks never ask for these via email.

📎

Unexpected Attachments

Suspicious file attachments (.exe, .zip, .scr). Even PDFs and documents can contain malware. Don't open if unexpected.

🎭

Generic Greetings

"Dear Customer" or "Dear User" instead of your name. Legitimate companies personalize their communications.

✍️

Grammar Errors

Spelling mistakes, awkward phrasing, and poor grammar. Legitimate companies maintain professional standards.

🌐

Mismatched Website

Website URL doesn't match the company name or has slight variations. Check HTTPS and padlock icon.

Types of Phishing Attacks

Different attack vectors and methods used by cybercriminals

Email Attack

Email Phishing

Mass emails sent to random recipients impersonating legitimate companies. Lowest sophistication but highest volume.

"Your Apple ID will be closed unless you verify..."
Targeted Attack

Spearphishing

Highly targeted attacks against specific individuals using personal information. Research-based and convincing.

"Hey John, can you approve this invoice from our vendor before you leave?"
CEO Attack

Whaling

Targets high-value individuals like executives. Impersonates senior leadership asking for urgent financial transfers.

"CEO here, can you discreetly wire $50K for a confidential transaction?"
Deception

Clone Phishing

Clones legitimate emails but replaces links/attachments with malicious versions. Appears authentic to recipients.

Duplicate of a real invoice with malicious payment details
Technical Attack

SMishing & Vishing

SMS text messages (SMishing) or voice calls (Vishing) pretending to be from banks or services requesting action.

"This is your bank. Confirm your card by calling 1-800-..."
Site Deception

Evil Twin Hotspots

Fake WiFi networks mimicking legitimate businesses that capture login credentials when users connect.

"CoffeeShop_Free" WiFi that's actually a attacker's network

Best Practices

👁️

Verify Senders

Always check the full email address and hover over links to see actual URLs before clicking.

⏱️

Don't Rush

Legitimate companies don't create urgent deadlines. Take time to verify suspicious requests through official channels.

🔐

Use Multi-Factor Authentication

Enable 2FA on all important accounts. Even if credentials are stolen, accounts remain protected.

🔄

Update Regularly

Keep software, OS, and security tools updated to patch vulnerabilities exploited by attackers.

🛡️

Use Security Tools

Deploy email filters, anti-malware software, and browser extensions that identify suspicious sites.

📞

Call Back Directly

When in doubt, call the organization using a number from their official website, not the email.

👨‍🏫

Stay Educated

Attend security awareness training and stay informed about new attack methods and threats.

🚫

Don't Share Passwords

No legitimate organization will ask for your password via email. Ever. It's never acceptable.

If You Suspect Phishing

Step-by-step response to minimize damage

1

Stop & Don't Click

Don't click any links or download attachments. Don't reply to the email. Your first instinct to verify is correct.

2

Verify Independently

Contact the organization using contact information from their official website, not from the suspicious email. Ask if they sent it.

3

Report to IT/Security

Forward the suspicious email to your organization's security team or IT department. They can investigate and block similar attacks.

4

Report to the Organization

Report phishing to the legitimate company being impersonated. Most have abuse/phishing@company.com addresses. They'll help others.

5

Change Your Password

If you entered credentials, change your password immediately from a clean computer. If you suspect a breach, contact your bank/provider.

6

Monitor Accounts

Watch your financial and email accounts for suspicious activity. Consider placing a fraud alert with credit bureaus if identity theft is suspected.

When In Doubt, Reach Out

It's always better to ask for verification than to fall victim to a phishing attack
